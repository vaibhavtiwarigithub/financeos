## 1. Fed Decisions (FOMC)

### 1a. Rate Hike (Hawkish surprise)

**Immediate reaction (0–2h after announcement, 2:00 PM ET):**
- SPY: typically -0.8% to -1.5% on first 30 min
- TLT (20Y bonds): -0.5% to -1.2% (yields rise = bond prices fall)
- GLD: -0.3% to -0.8% (real rates rising = gold headwind)
- USD (DXY): +0.3% to +0.8%
- Small caps (IWM) underperform large caps by ~0.4%

**Regime sensitivity:**
- Bear market: hike surprises cause outsized SPY drops (-2% to -3%)
- Bull market: market often "prices in" and rallies after initial dip if "last hike" narrative takes hold
- Choppy/neutral: moves mean-revert within 48h ~60% of the time

**Day+1 to Day+5 pattern:**
- If Fed is perceived as "done hiking" → SPY +1.5% to +3% over following week (relief rally)
- If further hikes expected → SPY flat to -1% over following week
- Growth stocks (QQQ) underperform value (IVE) by ~0.6%/week during sustained hiking cycles

**Key tell:** Watch the 2Y yield. If 2Y falls after a hike, market believes it's near the end → bullish. If 2Y rises → more hikes expected → bearish.

---

### 1b. Rate Cut (Dovish surprise)

**Immediate reaction:**
- SPY: +0.8% to +1.5% on first 30 min
- BUT: first cut often comes during deteriorating economic conditions → "bad news is bad news" risk
- If cut is "insurance cut" (economy still OK) → SPY +1.5% to +2.5% and sustains
- If cut is "recession cut" (economy weakening) → SPY rallies then fades within 3–5 days; eventual -5% to -15% over months

**Sector rotation on cuts:**
- Rate-sensitive (REIT, utilities, homebuilders) outperform +2% to +5% in week following first cut
- Banks underperform: net interest margin compresses
- Tech/growth outperform over 3-month horizon if economy is healthy

**Warning:** 2001 and 2007 both started with rate cuts. The initial SPY rally after each was a trap. Check leading indicators (ISM Manufacturing, initial jobless claims) before going long aggressively.

---

### 1c. Hold (as expected) — Powell presser matters

**The announcement itself is usually priced in.** Movement comes from:
- Press conference tone (hawkish language → -0.5% to -1%)
- Dot plot revisions (more hikes in dot plot = surprise hawkish → -0.8%)
- Balance sheet (QT pace changes)

**Pattern:** SPY often fades into FOMC day then rallies after if hold is confirmed (buy the rumor, sell the news inverted — "buy after the relief").

---

### 1d. Fed Chair Speech (Non-FOMC)

- Jackson Hole (late August): historically high volatility event; direction unpredictable but ±1.5% moves common
- Prepared testimony (Humphrey-Hawkins): lower impact; markets look for changes vs. previous testimony
- Unscripted comments: highest risk — can cause ±0.8% within minutes

## 2. CPI (Consumer Price Index) — released ~8:30 AM ET, ~2nd week of month

### Hot CPI (above consensus)

**Immediate:**
- SPY: -1.0% to -1.8% within 1h of release
- TLT: -0.8% to -1.5%
- GLD: -0.3% to -0.6%
- High-multiple tech (ARKK-style): -2% to -3%

**Duration:**
- If Fed has credibility and core PCE was already cooling: SPY recovers 50–80% of drop within 3 days
- If CPI surprises for 2nd consecutive month: SPY drops hold; no recovery for 5–10 days; re-prices rate path

**Key watch:** Core CPI (ex-food/energy) matters more than headline. A hot headline but cooling core = dip-buy opportunity. Hot core = real problem.

---

### Cool CPI (below consensus)

**Immediate:**
- SPY: +1.0% to +1.8%
- TLT: +0.5% to +1.2%
- Rate-sensitive sectors: +2% to +3%
- USD (DXY): -0.3% to -0.5%

**Duration:**
- 2022–2024 showed every "cool CPI" print led to sustained 1–2 week rally
- Growth/tech outperforms for 3–5 days after cool print

**Best trade setup:** Oversold growth stocks going into a cool CPI print historically produced 3–5% in the week following. High-risk/high-reward.

---

### In-line CPI (as expected)

- SPY moves < 0.4% on average
- Attention shifts to next catalyst (Fed meeting, jobs report)
- Pre-FOMC: in-line CPI often gives the Fed "permission" to hold → mildly bullish

## 3. Jobs Report (NFP — Non-Farm Payrolls) — 1st Friday of month, 8:30 AM ET

### Strong jobs (above consensus)

**Reaction depends heavily on regime:**

**During hiking cycle:** Strong jobs = Fed keeps hiking = BEARISH
- SPY: -0.5% to -1.2%
- "Goldilocks" is dead if jobs too hot

**During rate-cut anticipation:** Strong jobs = cuts delayed = BEARISH
- SPY: -0.8% to -1.5%
- 2Y yield rises 10–20 bps

**During early cut cycle (Fed has started cutting):** Strong jobs = soft landing confirmed = BULLISH
- SPY: +0.5% to +1.0%
- Cyclicals (XLY, XLI) outperform

---

### Weak jobs (below consensus)

**During hiking cycle:** Weak jobs = rate path lower = BULLISH (initially)
- SPY: +0.5% to +1.2% (but watch for "recession fear" if very weak)

**During recession fear:** Weak jobs = hard landing = VERY BEARISH
- SPY: -1.5% to -3%
- Key threshold: sub-100k is "fear territory"; sub-50k triggers recession pricing

**Unemployment rate is the tell:** Jobs can be "revised" next month. Unemployment rate rising (especially +0.3% from cycle low) is the most reliable recession signal.

---

### Goldilocks jobs (in-line)

- SPY typically flat to +0.3%
- Market digests without drama
- Often sets up a quiet week unless other catalysts

## 4. PCE (Personal Consumption Expenditures) — released ~end of month

Fed's preferred inflation measure. Similar reaction pattern to CPI but ~40% smaller magnitude because:
1. Released 3 weeks after CPI (market has already partially priced it)
2. Less media coverage = less retail reaction
3. Fed telegraphs reactions more (CPI is a surprise; PCE is predictable after CPI)

**Rule of thumb:** If CPI surprised significantly, PCE usually confirms direction. Trade size = 0.6× the CPI trade.

---

## 5. ISM Manufacturing PMI — 1st business day of month

- > 50 = expansion; < 50 = contraction
- SPY correlation: ~0.3 (weaker than jobs/CPI)
- **Most impactful:** New Orders sub-index (leading indicator) and Employment sub-index
- ISM < 45 for 3 consecutive months has historically preceded recessions

**Reliable pattern:** ISM Manufacturing bottoming and crossing back above 50 is a strong cyclical buy signal (especially industrials/materials). The turn is the trade, not the level.

---

## 6. Treasury Auctions

Often underappreciated. A poor 10Y or 30Y auction causes:
- 10Y yield spike of 3–8 bps
- SPY: -0.2% to -0.6%

**Setup:** When the US runs large deficits and foreign demand weakens, auction failures become more frequent. Watch bid-to-cover ratio: below 2.3× for 10Y = concern.

## Interaction Effects

| Sequence | Effect |
|---|---|
| Hot CPI → Fed hold | Confusing; markets often sell tech, buy value |
| Cool CPI → strong jobs | Net neutral; wait for resolution |
| Weak jobs → recession fear | Bonds rally; SPY falls; defensives outperform |
| Fed cuts → cool CPI | Strongest bull case; growth/tech best performers |
| Fed hikes → hot CPI × 3 | Maximum bearish pressure; 2022 replay risk |

---

## Calendar Clustering Risk

When multiple major events cluster (NFP + FOMC in same week), volatility compounds.
VIX typically spikes 15–25% going into such weeks. Reduce position sizes by 30% during clustered event weeks.
